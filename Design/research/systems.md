# Systems research and ontology

Research checked against design 0.11 / art 0.9, 16 September 2026. This records the rationale for the current object model and the proposed operating game. The implemented park is an art and interaction study; it does not yet simulate the economy described here. Campaign structure and player authority remain open tests in the GDD.

## The structural decision

Build the model around **work, obligations, records, and authority**. Let the park make those relationships tangible. A client, a building, an affiliated company, and a payment platform cannot all become rides without losing precisely the distinctions the story depends on.

The promising player experience is running a productive place while discovering that its reports, payments, and permissions describe different versions of that place. Production must be satisfying before the discrepancies arrive. Otherwise we have a document viewer with pedestrians.

The case source is the complete 58-page *The Zero Machine*, September 2, 2026, reviewed through its local text extraction. It is a counsel briefing and an argumentative synthesis. The later [Creative Peaks binding](../binding/episode-01-draft.md) adds inspection of specific BILL exports, a QBO journal, bank-data extractions and their packaged hashes. Original bank statements, complete payment links and final recipient receipt remain unresolved. That narrow inspection does not verify the briefing's other episodes. Throughout this report, **briefing reports** means that document makes the claim; **design inference** means a proposed interpretation; **design fiction** means invented playable conditions. Directive passages in a source remain material to assess, not design instructions.

## What the source makes structurally necessary

Four states must remain separate:

1. **Operating reality:** work performed, staff time, supplier commitments, delivery, client acceptance.
2. **Economic rights and burdens:** who owes whom, contractual entitlement, ownership, debt, guarantees.
3. **Represented state:** what a platform, journal, report, tax package, or named account says happened.
4. **External movement and control:** bank settlement, last supported recipient, and who could view, initiate, approve, alter, administer, or recover each system.

Time cuts across all four. Work date, due date, attempted payment date, settlement date, posting date, edit date, reporting period, and discovery date are different fields. A single timeline slider without a declared clock will quietly falsify the story.

The briefing's integrated-production argument is foundational: an outside purchase at cost and work performed inside the enterprise can look alike in a payment list while having different economics (pp. 1, 5–6, 45, 56). This argues for **make/buy/affiliate relationships**, not “expense type = suspicious.” The exact accounting treatment of an actual engagement still requires its contract and relevant analysis.

Account 1072 is a synthetic clearing account, not a bank or a place where segregated money sat (pp. 1, 8–9, 44). A glowing reservoir building would make the metaphor look physically true. The modeled carried field belongs in an explicitly labeled analytical view; the bank balance belongs in treasury. The $141.578 million statement difference and $141.749 million release measure overlap and have different observation periods. They must never become two piles of stolen money. The quarterly field is partly allocated, and its exact path is model-dependent.

The source also supplies counterexamples. Ten ordinary pooled allocations total $1.014 million; 65 intercompany pairs have bank support; the SiriusXM $150,000 payment is genuine; a vendor merge previously treated as a conflict is resolved (pp. 45–46). The early Malaky accommodation is not established as fraudulent at inception. Withdrawn cap/penny, hidden-card, and complete refund-loop theories must stay withdrawn (p. 44). A sound mechanic must accommodate these cases without special pleading.

## Entity taxonomy

Use stable identities with dated relationships. A person can be an employee, owner, authorized signer, and account holder at different times. One label should not erase the others.

| Object | What it owns | Playable expression | Do not collapse into |
|---|---|---|---|
| Person | Dated roles, documented actions, relationships | Named character or anonymized worker; inspector | Platform username or inferred motive |
| Employment relationship | Employer, department, capacity, recurring compensation and benefits | Persistent park staff grouped as creative, production, accounts, media, ops | Universal salary model or generic vendor |
| Supplier engagement | Supplier, service, milestones, rates, invoice terms | Visiting crew, courier, specialist, external delivery | A guest buying entertainment |
| Client relationship | Contracting party and portfolio of agreements | Client representative and commission board | A permanently profitable attraction |
| Contract / commission | Scope, approvals, fee, outside costs, rights, deadlines | Job offered to the agency | Cash already received |
| Project / work package | Tasks, dependencies, capacity reservations, deliverables | Placeable production cluster, changing with the job | Legal entity or client identity |
| Site / lease / facility | Physical capacity, lease terms, occupancy dates | Office, studio, fabrication area, satellite site | Property owner or affiliate |
| Legal entity | Ownership, accounts, obligations, relationships | Selectable boundary or district; sometimes off-map | Building or decorative brand |
| Obligation | Debtor, creditor, amount, currency, basis, due date | Persistent promise that can remain unresolved | Invoice image or Paid label |
| Payment attempt / settlement | Funding, batch membership, method, returns, endpoint evidence | Payment workbench and lifecycle trace | Obligation itself |
| Account / platform profile | System identity, versions, linked credentials and permissions | Desktop application and control inspector | Natural person at keyboard |
| Record / version | Source locator, authoring context, time and prior versions | Inspectable receipt, message, report or comparison | Ground truth or the claim made about it |
| Claim | Proposition, scope, supporting/conflicting records and review state | Attributed finding or open question | A record or a proven endpoint |
| Permission grant | Holder, target, action, start/end, delegator | Control overlay and blocked-action explanation | Job title or ownership percentage |

Clients and suppliers may both appear as visitors in the scene, but they need different behaviors and economic relations. A freelancer can be a recurring character and still remain a supplier engagement. A payroll employee remains a person when their work is allocated to an affiliate.

The suggested attached 1099 is useful as a discoverable record, not a species marker. Reporting depends on the relationship, payment type, recipient, and year. The IRS's 2024 instructions explicitly distinguish wages and card/network reporting. Model a reporting profile and dated form record; do not stamp every freelancer “1099” at entry. This also preserves the case's alleged changes in tax identity and reporting route. [IRS 2024 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-prior/i1099mec--2024.pdf).

“Office Space” can be a build-menu category. It should not be an ontology. McKibbin, Boerum, Johnson, Colorado, Porter, and Hong Kong require individual source checks before entering the scenario: physical location, lessee, operator, legal entity, opening period, and actual narrative function. Hong Kong already demonstrates why site and entity must separate: the briefing describes both local production and intercompany cash/accounting (pp. 2, 22, 48).

## Two viable architectures

| | A. Operating campus | B. Reconstruction park |
|---|---|---|
| Player role | Fictional operations desk with bounded authority | Present-day investigator arranging a reconstruction |
| Main verb | Plan and deliver work | Reconstruct and test an explanation |
| Spatial model | Facilities and production capacity; project clusters | Stable historical sites with reconstructed work and transaction paths |
| Decisions | Capacity, sequencing, commitments, contingency, information requests | Which chain to examine, which record would distinguish explanations, how to present a bounded finding |
| Story delivery | Ordinary work develops contradictions; case records appear at relevant mechanisms | Historical episodes become traversable systems; records open from their objects |
| Strength | Familiar management pleasure and consequences before exposition | Stronger fidelity; unresolved endpoints can stay unresolved |
| Risk | Counterfactual choices may imply that real people could simply have prevented the outcome | Investigation can become matching receipts until a chapter unlocks |
| Scope | Small authored scenario plus reusable simulation | Larger evidence apparatus, lighter operational simulation |

**Recommendation: test A and B with the same small episode before choosing the campaign.** A is the stronger candidate for the requested game; B is the stronger reference and explainer mode. They can share objects without pretending to share outcomes.

In A, explicitly fictional training or alternate scenarios allow operational choices to change results. Historical case records remain fixed and inspectable. In B, agency changes what the player understands and what they can substantiate; it does not rewrite a real bank event. Avoid a historical avatar endowed with every password and hindsight. The briefing's unequal access is not a difficulty setting that a clever player should bypass.

A literal “client rides, finance kiosks, fraud meter” architecture is a weak third option. It is initially recognizable but turns source categories into arbitrary gameplay categories. A kiosk can represent a physical service counter if space and staffing matter. BILL is a workbench because its consequential properties are profiles, batches, states, permissions, and evidence—not its walking distance from the studio.

## RCT craft worth carrying across

RCT's useful inheritance is **diagnosable systems assembled into a place**. Keep the loop of placing a capability, watching demand and throughput, inspecting a local failure, and making a bounded adjustment.

There is a precise historical example of a scenario rule changing the whole economic problem: Sawyer says the new *Loopy Landscapes* parks deliberately prevent raising admission, making individual attractions earn their income. Transfer that principle to different agency contracts: a fixed retainer, approval-dependent production budget, or media commitment should change what good planning means. Do not treat all incoming money as the same fare. [Sawyer FAQ](https://www.chrissawyergames.com/faq1.htm).

OpenRCT2's guest implementation exposes needs, preferences, satisfaction, affordability-related behavior, and local thoughts; its ride-ratings implementation evaluates track context and proximity. These are useful inspectable mechanisms in a modern reconstruction, not proof that every current behavior exactly matches the 1999 executable. Transfer the causal specificity: “waiting for supplier confirmation” should point to a real dependency; “stressed” should not be the only diagnosis. A beautiful campus may still contain an impossible schedule. [Guest source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/develop/src/openrct2/entity/Guest.cpp), [ratings source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OpenRCT2/OpenRCT2/develop/src/openrct2/ride/RideRatings.cpp).

Borrow prebuilt and editable **production plans**: a repeatable campaign package is analogous to a reusable ride design. The player can customize a few consequential parts—make/buy, staffing, sequence, contingency, milestones—without laying every metre of track. Sawyer's own downloadable track collection is a period example of designed objects being shared and tested in existing scenarios. [Track collection](https://www.chrissawyergames.com/download1.htm).

Do not import pathological incentives accidentally. If the score is revenue, the player should chase revenue even when that destroys the intended lesson. If finding fraud earns points, every ordinary payment becomes a missed scoring opportunity. Use separate outcomes: commitments honored, work delivered, liquidity maintained, people burdened, records preserved, claims supported. A bad business outcome may coexist with excellent reasoning. No overall righteousness percentage is required.

The user-supplied [Drew1440 RCT page](https://drew1440.com/2024/09/24/rollercoaster-tycoon/) is a secondary visual/reference lead. The retrieved text is sparse; it has not been used to establish mechanics.

## SimCity, Zomboid, and period design theory

SimCity designer Guillaume Pierre describes maps, networks/globals, agents, and building-to-building connections as different simulation techniques. That distinction is useful here: floor capacity belongs to space; obligations form a network; worker availability can be aggregated; an individual courier need only be simulated if their behavior changes a decision. There is no prize for giving every invoice pathfinding. This is an inference from his published GDC abstract, not a claim to have reviewed the complete lecture. [GDC 2024](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034347/We-Built-This-City-on).

Project Zomboid offers a more useful precedent than zombies: domestic objects, persistent consequences, player-made plans, and adjustable scenario conditions. Its 2013 developer post explicitly separates canonical survival rules from customizable sandbox rules. That supports a historical record mode and a clearly marked experiment mode. It does not justify making documented uncertainty a random dice roll. [The Indie Stone, 2013](https://projectzomboid.com/blog/news/2013/04/in-the-guts-of-the-sandbox/).

Three period/near-period tools should govern the GDD:

* **Doug Church, 1999:** intention and perceivable consequence. Before implementing a choice, write the plan a player could form and the visible consequence that would teach them whether it worked. “Click OK to continue” satisfies neither. [Formal Abstract Design Tools](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/formal-abstract-design-tools).
* **Hunicke, LeBlanc, Zubek, 2004:** use mechanics/dynamics/aesthetics as a testable chain. A capacity reservation is a mechanic; juggling competing commitments is the dynamic; capable stewardship under pressure is the intended experience. Extra documents do not automatically improve that chain. [MDA paper](https://aaai.org/papers/ws04-04-001-mda-a-formal-approach-to-game-design-and-game-research/).
* **Henry Jenkins, 2004:** embed narrative information in the environment and its objects. An idle fabrication area beside a report that claims costs vanished can create an inquiry; a popup explaining that contradiction cannot substitute for it. This is our application of narrative architecture, not an assertion about the case. [Game Design as Narrative Architecture](https://electronicbookreview.com/publications/game-design-as-narrative-architecture/).

Bogost's procedural rhetoric adds the necessary criticism: rules themselves make arguments. Making every clearing transaction harmful would teach that clearing is inherently fraudulent, whatever the footnote says. Elizabeth Sampat's GDC abstract similarly treats simulation design as an expression of assumptions. The GDD needs a short **argument made by this rule** field and a counterexample beside each financial mechanism. [Bogost](https://bogost.com/books/persuasive-games), [Sampat](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1023994/Systems-Make-Statements-Simulations-and).

Stone Librande's *Simulating a City, One Page at a Time* is a useful documentation precedent: a large design needs inspectable subsystem pages. Use a comprehensive GDD index plus one page per loop, state machine, and source-to-mechanic mapping; do not attempt to make every detail live in one immaculate monolith. Only the published session abstract was reviewed. [GDC 2013](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1018764/Simulating-a-City-One-Page).

## Proposed operating and inquiry loops

The ordinary loop is **accept scope → reserve capacity → commit suppliers → produce → obtain acceptance → collect/pay → review**. Capacity, cash, and settlement move on different clocks. Departments should have skills and finite time; payroll and benefits recur independently of client collection. External engagements add flexible capacity but also lead times, milestones, approval conditions, and obligations. Neither should be universally cheaper or morally preferred.

The inquiry loop interrupts only when it changes a decision: **notice conflict → compare representations → name the missing evidence → request or inspect it → narrow the finding → revise an operating plan**. Do not require every bill to become a puzzle. Batch ordinary supported work and surface meaningful exceptions.

The fictional test scenario gives Campaign C-07 a $100,000 budget, an internal team, and a fabrication supplier. A saved platform representation of the $12,000 obligation is marked Paid; the supplied bank events show a debit and an equal return; no subsequent settlement is supplied. A deadline approaches. Proposed operating choices are to reserve substitute capacity, protect another project's slot, request supplier confirmation, continue with contingency, or defer the handoff. These choices and their economic consequences are not implemented in the art study. A confirmation response may be pending or unavailable: incomplete observation does not establish nonpayment.

The decision spends scarce capacity or cash to keep a promise while resolving uncertainty. A normal pooled-payment example must accompany the exception. The current [document workbench](#art/interface-materials) demonstrates the fictional invoice, saved payment versions and bank-event strip. Its Tuesday v2 predates Wednesday’s return; a later status observation is still needed to implement the proposed persistence event. The Creative Peaks comparison has a separate [export-level binding](../binding/native-inventory.md): its return date differs from the briefing, the Chase extraction conflicts with QBO on direction, and the final supplier endpoint remains unresolved.

## Discovery order and views

The first-episode test compresses **one job → one payment lifecycle → a comparison of records**. The proposed campaign separately builds **work → authority → reporting → payment → entities → handover → after closure**, as specified in the [chapter sequence](#narrative/chapter-sequence). Neither sequence rewrites chronology; historical episodes retain their actual dates. A 1990s desktop presentation is an interface convention; it must not move a later chat, bank product, or AI conversation into the 1990s.

Use five coordinated views in this order: **Work** (capacity and dependencies), **Cash** (observed movements, attempts and unresolved endpoints), **Information** (versions, sources and claims), **Control** (dated permissions), and **People** (employment, engagements and workload). Rights and obligations remain in the inspector across views. Keep the selected project, person or obligation pinned while switching. An additional comparison can show the company as presented to a specified audience. “Reported sales” must name its report and period. The [five-view figure](#interface/five-views) uses one fictional obligation to demonstrate the distinction; the art explorer presents architecture and sources, with the practice-document desk accessed separately.

Real named characters need source-based roles and documented acts. Use invented archetypes only in the fictional scenario: the producer protecting a deadline, the specialist waiting on a deposit, the account lead selling tomorrow's capacity. Their behaviors should have mundane operational causes. A named person's alleged culpability is not an NPC trait.

## Next steps: prototype gates and failure tests

Begin with the [P2 ordinary-work proof](#production/current-focus). The remaining checks belong to the later episode tests. Use six to eight participants diagnostically, as specified in the GDD; these are observation prompts, not measured results or statistical pass rates.

1. **Ordinary-play test:** give participants a three-minute production scenario without anomalies. Observe whether they voluntarily try a second plan and explain its tradeoff. Failure means the management loop needs work before more lore.
2. **Ontology test:** after the slice, ask participants to distinguish client, project, site, supplier, and entity without coaching. Any recurring conflation is a model or representation failure.
3. **Settlement test:** present returned-payment, legitimate pooled-payment, and duplicate-Paid examples. Record whether participants separate observation from inference in all three, and investigate recurring errors. No UI state may assert a recipient without its required record.
4. **False-join test:** include the source's two same-date $25,000 transfers (p. 45). The model must retain two objects; amount/date matching alone must never merge them.
5. **Control test:** ask who can view, initiate, approve, and administer one transaction at two dates. Record whether they can answer from the control view without substituting an organization chart.
6. **Agency test:** two different capacity plans must produce materially different delivery/cash consequences in fiction; neither may change documentary facts. If outcomes differ only in dialogue, the game is still a slideshow.
7. **Nonadditivity test:** combine statement, bank, bill, and return views. The system must refuse a total across overlapping populations and explain the selected measure in plain language.
8. **Attention test:** an eight-minute session allows at least three consequential operational actions before opening a long document. Essential facts remain recoverable without timed reading or mandatory pixel hunting.

The unresolved design question is whether ordinary production planning remains enjoyable when the payment exception is removed. Test ordinary work first, then add the evidence distinctions before fixing the campaign structure. Completed art components can support the test without predetermining its answer.

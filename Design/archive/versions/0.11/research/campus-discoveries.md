# Campus discoveries — objects with evidence

Research checked against design 0.11 / art 0.9, 16 September 2026. Sources: inspected photographs in Matt Fry’s locally archived public portfolio, checked against the public project pages and PSF Projects’ project page. These are visual references, not facts about the case. Each entry separates the observed object, its current game interpretation and any further proposed interaction.

The sculpted reception desk, indoor Chrysler and tree under the skylight supply three different silhouettes and spatial conditions: arrival, an obstruction people gather around, and a shared clearing. The park makes the car a circuit attraction and the room name Supernova an orbital pavilion. The inflatable/shark kit changes the courtyard arrangement. Small objects can reward attention through their shape and placement without requiring a dialogue box each.

**Reading rule:** “Observed” describes the photograph. “Reported” comes from a named source. “Proposal” is new game design. Proposed game behaviours do not claim what the real office or its occupants did.

## 1. The sculpted white reception desk — Porter

**Observed.** A very long, irregular white counter has rounded bulges, scooped concavities and a taller right end. Its surface is densely flecked with charcoal fragments. Plants cluster in the recesses along its foot. Two screens sit against a tall timber wall behind it; a large, shallow, white pendant hangs above. Workshop pictures show the same sculptural form supported on timber, then being worked by hand. The exact compound/material is not established by appearance.

**Evidence.** [Installed front desk](../assets/office-references/porter-reception.jpg); [workshop state](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1739922190330-8PTENPE6N4X5VDFXUDUY/IMG_0879.jpg); [fabrication in progress](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1739922191235-A1SOT0D2E4RHWGKZQ2QN/IMG_0891.jpg); [surface close-up](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1739922435406-PBVE3GGFD6PWVO0A7DYP/IMG_0964.jpg).

**Reported.** Matt’s page groups this sequence under “Front desk.” His page describes his involvement in design, fabrication management and art; it does not assign the fabrication of this particular object to a named person. [Matt Fry, 65 Porter](https://mattfryed.com/65porter).

**Current study.** L-07 makes the counter an open-air arrival landmark, using unequal heights, curved lobes, shaded hollows and sparse surface flecks. The source's exact material is not assigned. **Next steps:** test a visitor queue along the concave face and a receptionist visible behind it; construction stages remain proposed.

![Current sculpted reception sprite](../assets/sprite-catalogue/objects/L-07/default.png)

**Credit.** Portfolio source: Matt Fry. Photographer and object-specific maker not identified in the inspected source.

## 2. A full-sized white Chrysler indoors — Johnson

**Observed.** A long white four-door car sits across the office’s circulation space. Chrome trim, red rear lamps, a dark cabin and four round front lamps make it readable from both ends. The Chrysler lettering is visible. It appears with desks, a mezzanine and orange stair; a second image shows the front with an inflatable castle behind it. This establishes presence, not ownership, operating condition or why it was there.

**Evidence.** [Rear three-quarter view](../assets/office-references/johnson-mezzanine.jpg); [front view](../assets/office-references/johnson-inflatable.jpg). A white car is also partly visible in [Porter’s kitchen view](../assets/office-references/porter-gathering.jpg); the photographs alone do not establish that it is the same vehicle.

**Current study.** L-01 is a long white sedan with 16 orientations, sloped glazing, recessed wheels and selective chrome. It can remain parked or run the invented L-10 circuit with a planted island and boarding apron. The full assembly makes the theme-park interpretation visible. It carries no financial symbolism. Pedestrian gathering, seating and ride boarding are not simulated.

![Current invented Chrysler attraction assembly](../assets/sprite-catalogue/objects/L-10/default.png)

**Credit.** Johnson images: Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not explicitly credited on the project page. The filename “JoeCarrotta” is retained as provenance, not treated as a verified authorship statement. Porter kitchen image: Zach Pontz, matched to PSF’s gallery.

## 3. Inflatable castle and suspended shark — Johnson

**Observed.** A red/yellow/blue/green inflatable castle occupies the end of the hall, directly behind the Chrysler. Its four pointed corner turrets reach towards the rafters. A large, dark shark-shaped balloon hangs below a skylight. Small coloured balloons are caught high among the roof members; others lie at floor level. Bunting runs along the orange mezzanine railing.

**Evidence.** [Daytime view of castle, shark and car](../assets/office-references/johnson-inflatable.jpg); [occupied evening view](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1588805943464-SBZMVEB3NV96UJKMRP5C/JoeCarrotta-0177.jpg).

**Reported.** The project page includes these photographs without an event caption or date. Do not infer that the inflatable was a permanent office facility, or that this is the event described in entry 5. [Matt Fry, 266 Johnson](https://mattfryed.com/266-johnson).

**Current study.** The courtyard switches between booths, garden and inflatable layouts. The castle has rounded inflated columns, seams and entrance netting; the separately drawn shark has a restrained floating motion. This is an authored layout switch, not a simulation of inflation, crowd arrival or path congestion. **Next steps:** test whether a temporary event should reserve floor space or change physical routes in the operating game.

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio. Original filenames contain JoeCarrotta; project page supplies no explicit photographer credit.

## 4. The tabby in the sun patch — Johnson

**Observed.** A brown tabby lies on the floor with its head bent towards a forepaw. It has a purple collar with purple and green tags. The orange stair and desk chairs remain visible behind it; a hard-edged patch of sunlight cuts across the floor. No name or ownership is established. Do not transcribe small contact text from the collar.

**Evidence.** [Cat under the skylight](../assets/office-references/johnson-cat.jpg).

**Current study.** L-06 has a resting profile, forepaws, lowered head, tabby marks, purple collar and four sampled tail poses. It remains readable with motion paused. Paw-washing, loafing and walking between sun patches are not implemented. **Next steps:** compare its scenic scale with nearby staff and decide whether additional ambient states add enough to justify them. It has no task marker, dialogue or case-solving role.

![Resting tabby from the current catalogue](../assets/sprite-catalogue/objects/L-06/tail-0.png)

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not identified in the inspected source.

## 5. The arcade cabinet beside the car — Johnson

**Observed.** A substantial upright arcade cabinet has a dotted illuminated header and bright vertical light strips. The car sits immediately behind it. A projected game screen on the far wall shows two sides of a court and a 1–1 score. Several people face the cabinet. Cabinet title lettering is partly obscured.

**Evidence.** [Cabinet, car and projected game](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1588805354828-5TRANSSCPTBHG8UIGZNS/Screen+Shot+2020-05-06+at+6.13.54+PM.png).

**Reported.** Matt captions this image “Volleygon Launch Party.” That supports the event’s identity; the screenshot filename is not the event date. [Matt Fry, 266 Johnson](https://mattfryed.com/266-johnson).

**Next steps — proposed, not implemented.** A small optional playable diversion could occupy a cabinet, with a scene-scale light response and a short queue. It should be entered deliberately and exited immediately. An original tiny game could preserve the observed relationship between game, gathering and architecture without reproducing Volleygon or importing its art.

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio. Photographer and depicted cabinet’s creators are not established by this photograph/caption.

## 6. Black stag with bright antlers — Porter

**Observed.** A nearly life-sized black stag stands at the end of an open desk aisle. Its bright silver-looking antlers extend widely against the pale wall; four thin legs and a compact dark body give it a very different profile from an employee. A mint-coloured manual typewriter also sits on the near cabinet in the same image.

**Evidence.** [Stag and mint typewriter](../assets/office-references/porter-hall.jpg); [stag through glazing](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1732580662631-R8PD63I3ANVKFOG8ADZC/2-Capture0003-307.jpg).

**Current study.** L-02 enlarges the stag into a fixed park landmark on an invented plinth. The near-frontal dark body, separated legs and bright branching antlers carry its identity. **Next steps:** assess the silhouette with surrounding people and planting; the mint typewriter remains an undrawn incidental-object candidate.

**Reported / credit.** The sculpture’s maker, title and reason for placement are not given. Photos: Zach Pontz, matched by image filename and content to [PSF’s gallery](https://www.psfprojects.com/workplace/madwell-creative-agency).

## 7. Pink circular neon with an animal outline — Porter

**Observed.** A pink circular neon sign encloses a white outlined animal on a white wall above a low ramp/partition. Its reflection appears in adjacent black-framed glazing. The exact animal and the sign’s maker are not securely identifiable at the available viewing scale.

**Evidence.** [Neon above the ramp](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1732580668856-HTBIUCN5YMXMSUINLIG2/Capture0022-126.jpg).

**Next steps — proposed, not implemented.** A compact wayfinding light could use a pink ring, a small pale interior mark and a single tinted wall patch. It could orient the player in the roof-off view. The animal, maker and meaning remain unresolved; the sign should not acquire an invented departmental identity.

**Credit.** Photograph: Zach Pontz via PSF/Matt Fry project galleries. Sign authorship and meaning unverified.

## 8. A tree occupying the skylight — Johnson

**Observed.** A tall, comparatively airy deciduous-looking tree rises from a large white rectangular planter within the desk floor. Its upper branches meet the skylight’s light opening. The raised planter has a stepped white edge. The tree, planter, skylight and surrounding chairs make one spatial arrangement; it is not a generic outdoor park tree transplanted indoors.

**Evidence.** [Tree, white planter and roof opening](../assets/office-references/johnson-mezzanine.jpg); [opposite view beside the bar](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1588805933333-S98Z2SA4VW4MZMOZJ6H5/6A0A9253.jpg).

**Reported.** Porter separately contains an indoor garden that PSF calls the Solarium, beside the kitchen and eating area. That is a different site and should not be used to name Johnson’s tree. [PSF Projects](https://www.psfprojects.com/workplace/madwell-creative-agency).

**Current study.** Johnson’s cutaway contains a tree in a white planter; Porter’s Solarium becomes a separate invented glass pavilion. These source associations remain distinct. **Next steps:** test an airy indoor canopy, the aligned roof opening and floor light together; a pause point or meeting use remains a proposed operating behavior. The current Johnson interior uses a white birch, following supplied project context. The photographic observation alone does not identify the species or establish the exact planting arrangement.

**Credit.** Johnson photographs: Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not established. PSF source supplies Porter’s Solarium description, not Johnson’s tree identification.

## 9. Coral brackets and white plywood dividers — Porter

**Observed.** A close-up shows white-faced plywood panels with exposed layered edges, a large circular hole, a coral-pink metal bracket with rounded ends and visible screws, and a slotted white metal floor support. Wide photographs show white cabinet/divider runs, circular black pulls, coloured upright posts and desk plants.

**Evidence.** [Joinery close-up](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1739921816128-NF79EUPODKVFWZZDB6OT/E0D74F78-3861-4549-8A54-9140639C361B.JPG); [assembled desk floor](../assets/office-references/porter-hall.jpg).

**Reported.** PSF credits Millwright with collaborative millwork design/fabrication. Matt credits his own design and fabrication-management involvement. These broad credits do not identify the author of this one bracket.

**Current study.** M-01 combines a white slab, warm ply edge, coloured supports, screens and separate chairs. The component can repeat along a row. **Next steps:** test cabinet/divider variants and an upgrade that adds one bay without breaking the occupied group. The photographed bracket remains a construction reference; it is not a decorative border applied throughout the interface.

**Credit.** Close-up photographer unspecified in Matt’s source. Wide desk-floor photograph: Zach Pontz. Architecture/millwork credits retained above.

## 10. Named rooms, saturated recesses and large paintings — Porter

**Observed.** The SUPERNOVA sign sits above a black-framed glazed meeting-room door. Amber curtains and a low broad pendant sit inside; a large painting of tightly packed, brightly coloured ghost-like figures hangs beside it and reflects in the glass. Elsewhere a MAGMA sign marks an orange-red recess; GROVE is visible on another room. The room names come from readable signage, not filenames.

**Evidence.** [SUPERNOVA, curtain and painting](../assets/office-references/porter-supernova.jpg); [MAGMA recess](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1732580664767-V1LUQQFL74X7NSRVDE4P/5-Capture0001-163.jpg); [GROVE doorway](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1732580667965-9KM29A8YNSQ29UIXGV7H/Capture0019-102.jpg). [Coral booth sequence](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/5eb3104d0e74237ec948380b/1732580665943-FDO8SVBWFTZA4QLA0FY7/8-Capture0001-251.jpg) adds small portrait blocks and black/white globe pendants to the same room-scale vocabulary.

**Current study.** Timber-room M-02 retains the coloured doorway pocket. Supernova becomes L-03, a black-and-silver roadside pavilion with ribbed lower panels, a chrome canopy, bright fascia lettering and an invented silver orbit around a warm sphere. **Next steps:** decide whether meeting occupancy warrants a curtain state. Paintings, portraits and other named rooms are not implemented as a complete room set; photographed art must not be imported as game texture.

## Current park study

The assembled map now uses Boerum, Porter and Johnson-derived building families. It includes original sprites for the counter, Chrysler, stag, Supernova pavilion, Solarium, orange overlook, tabby and inflatable/shark event kit. The inspector shows a source photograph beside the proposed use.

The parked car and optional miniature circuit are both available; the circuit is explicitly invented. Booths, garden and inflatable are alternative courtyard layouts, not a historical sequence. The Volleygon cabinet, neon and typewriter remain proposed additions.

## Next steps: scene curation

Use these checks where they support the [P2 ordinary-work proof](#production/current-focus). Extra objects and alternate layouts are optional studies, not prerequisites for that proof.

- **Three major anchors:** sculpted counter at arrival, car/circuit, and tree/planter. Check that each remains legible with labels hidden; test the indoor car placement separately from the implemented outdoor attraction.
- **One changing occupation:** inflatable/shark party kit or arcade gathering, not every observed event at once.
- **Quiet discoveries:** retain the implemented tabby and stag; compare the proposed neon and mint typewriter before expanding the set.
- **Repeatable structure:** white industrial frame, timber meeting-room volumes, saturated recesses, coral joinery, white desks. Avoid spreading the strongest object colours evenly across every building.
- **Chronology stays explicit:** Johnson’s orange exposed mezzanine stair, tree and party photos do not prove these same arrangements at Porter. Porter has its own enclosed orange stair, skylit social area and sculpted reception.
- **No trailer verified:** none of the 34 Porter and 11 Johnson gallery files inspected here establishes a trailer. A trailer would require another source or be labelled an original addition.

Use original sprite interpretation of these observed forms. Photographs and depicted artworks are retained as credited research sources; the archive does not itself establish a production reuse licence.

# Campus discoveries — objects with evidence

Research for the next campus pass. 16 September 2026. Sources: inspected photographs in Matt Fry’s locally archived public portfolio, checked against his current project pages and PSF Projects’ project page. These are visual references, not facts about the case.

Start with the sculpted reception desk, the indoor Chrysler, and the tree under the skylight. They produce three different silhouettes and three useful spatial conditions: arrival, an obstruction people gather around, and a shared clearing. The inflatable castle and shark balloon can transform that same space temporarily. Small objects should reward attention without requiring a dialogue box each.

**Reading rule:** “Observed” describes the photograph. “Reported” comes from a named source. “Proposal” is new game design. Proposed game behaviours do not claim what the real office or its occupants did.

## 1. The sculpted white reception desk — Porter

**Observed.** A very long, irregular white counter has rounded bulges, scooped concavities and a taller right end. Its surface is densely flecked with charcoal fragments. Plants cluster in the recesses along its foot. Two screens sit against a tall timber wall behind it; a large, shallow, white pendant hangs above. Workshop pictures show the same sculptural form supported on timber, then being worked by hand. The exact compound/material is not established by appearance.

**Evidence.** [Installed front desk](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/32-img_0947.jpg>); [workshop state](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/30-img_0879.jpg>); [fabrication in progress](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/31-img_0891.jpg>); [surface close-up](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/34-img_0964.jpg>).

**Reported.** Matt’s page groups this sequence under “Front desk.” His page describes his involvement in design, fabrication management and art; it does not assign the fabrication of this particular object to a named person. [Matt Fry, 65 Porter](https://mattfryed.com/65porter).

**Proposal.** Make this the arrival counter rather than inventing a ticket hut. At native pixel scale, use three or four broad lobes, two deep grey undercuts and very sparse dark flecks. Let visitors queue along its concave face while the receptionist remains visible behind it. A construction state can progress from support trestles to unfinished shell to occupied desk. Do not make the material shimmer.

**Credit.** Portfolio source: Matt Fry. Photographer and object-specific maker not identified in the inspected source.

## 2. A full-sized white Chrysler indoors — Johnson

**Observed.** A long white four-door car sits across the office’s circulation space. Chrome trim, red rear lamps, a dark cabin and four round front lamps make it readable from both ends. The Chrysler lettering is visible. It appears with desks, a mezzanine and orange stair; a second image shows the front with an inflatable castle behind it. This establishes presence, not ownership, operating condition or why it was there.

**Evidence.** [Rear three-quarter view](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/01-6a0a9246.jpg>); [front view](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/07-joecarrotta-0004.jpg>). A white car is also partly visible in [Porter’s kitchen view](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/16-9-capture0016-245.jpg>); the photographs alone do not establish that it is the same vehicle.

**Proposal.** Keep the car stationary and oversized relative to a desk. Give it a roughly two-tile length, four wheel anchors, a dark glass band and two-pixel chrome highlights. People route around it, stop beside it or briefly sit nearby. Turning it into a placeable indoor landmark is already sufficiently odd; it needs no invented financial symbolism or unexplained driving loop.

**Credit.** Johnson images: Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not explicitly credited on the project page. The filename “JoeCarrotta” is retained as provenance, not treated as a verified authorship statement. Porter kitchen image: Zach Pontz, matched to PSF’s gallery.

## 3. Inflatable castle and suspended shark — Johnson

**Observed.** A red/yellow/blue/green inflatable castle occupies the end of the hall, directly behind the Chrysler. Its four pointed corner turrets reach towards the rafters. A large, dark shark-shaped balloon hangs below a skylight. Small coloured balloons are caught high among the roof members; others lie at floor level. Bunting runs along the orange mezzanine railing.

**Evidence.** [Daytime view of castle, shark and car](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/07-joecarrotta-0004.jpg>); [occupied evening view](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/06-joecarrotta-0177.jpg>).

**Reported.** The project page includes these photographs without an event caption or date. Do not infer that the inflatable was a permanent office facility, or that this is the event described in entry 5. [Matt Fry, 266 Johnson](https://mattfryed.com/266-johnson).

**Proposal.** A temporary event kit is a stronger use than a permanent castle building: the floor clears, the inflatable rises, then people fill the newly constrained paths. One-pixel alternating turret compression suggests an inflated object. The shark can move slowly within a small tethered area, never follow the pointer. Ordinary work continuing under a shark is enough.

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio. Original filenames contain JoeCarrotta; project page supplies no explicit photographer credit.

## 4. The tabby in the sun patch — Johnson

**Observed.** A brown tabby lies on the floor with its head bent towards a forepaw. It has a purple collar with purple and green tags. The orange stair and desk chairs remain visible behind it; a hard-edged patch of sunlight cuts across the floor. No name or ownership is established. Do not transcribe small contact text from the collar.

**Evidence.** [Cat under the skylight](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/08-795b4469.jpg>).

**Proposal.** A quiet ambient inhabitant, with three readable states: loaf, paw-wash, short walk to another sun patch. The silhouette matters more than stripes: long low back, triangular ear, curled foreleg, one darker tail edge. Keep it approximately five to seven native pixels high, below a person’s knee. The scene should continue to work when animation is paused. No task marker, speech bubble or mystery-solving animal.

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not identified in the inspected source.

## 5. The arcade cabinet beside the car — Johnson

**Observed.** A substantial upright arcade cabinet has a dotted illuminated header and bright vertical light strips. The car sits immediately behind it. A projected game screen on the far wall shows two sides of a court and a 1–1 score. Several people face the cabinet. Cabinet title lettering is partly obscured.

**Evidence.** [Cabinet, car and projected game](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/10-screen-shot-2020-05-06-at-6.13.54-pm.png>).

**Reported.** Matt captions this image “Volleygon Launch Party.” That supports the event’s identity; the screenshot filename is not the event date. [Matt Fry, 266 Johnson](https://mattfryed.com/266-johnson).

**Proposal.** A small optional playable diversion can occupy the cabinet, with a simple scene-scale light response and a short two-person queue. It should be entered deliberately and exited immediately. The current game need not reproduce Volleygon or import its art. An original tiny game, labelled as a design proposal, would preserve the observed relationship between game, gathering and architecture.

**Credit.** Matt Fry portfolio. Photographer and depicted cabinet’s creators are not established by this photograph/caption.

## 6. Black stag with bright antlers — Porter

**Observed.** A nearly life-sized black stag stands at the end of an open desk aisle. Its bright silver-looking antlers extend widely against the pale wall; four thin legs and a compact dark body give it a very different profile from an employee. A mint-coloured manual typewriter also sits on the near cabinet in the same image.

**Evidence.** [Stag and mint typewriter](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/11-4-capture0005-142.jpg>); [stag through glazing](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/09-2-capture0003-307.jpg>).

**Proposal.** Use the stag as a fixed aisle-end landmark. Allocate enough negative space to read the antlers, using pale branches with one dark underside rather than a flickering spark effect. The little mint typewriter can be an incidental desk object using four mint pixels, a dark carriage and a pale paper square. Neither needs to become a menu.

**Reported / credit.** The sculpture’s maker, title and reason for placement are not given. Photos: Zach Pontz, matched by image filename and content to [PSF’s gallery](https://www.psfprojects.com/workplace/madwell-creative-agency).

## 7. Pink circular neon with an animal outline — Porter

**Observed.** A pink circular neon sign encloses a white outlined animal on a white wall above a low ramp/partition. Its reflection appears in adjacent black-framed glazing. The exact animal and the sign’s maker are not securely identifiable at the available viewing scale.

**Evidence.** [Neon above the ramp](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/20-capture0022-126.jpg>).

**Proposal.** Keep this a compact wayfinding light in the actual room geometry, with a pink ring and a small pale interior mark. A single tinted patch on the wall will read better than blur or animated flicker. In the roof-off view it can orient the player to the ramp side of the building. Do not relabel it a cat or invent a departmental logo.

**Credit.** Photograph: Zach Pontz via PSF/Matt Fry project galleries. Sign authorship and meaning unverified.

## 8. A tree occupying the skylight — Johnson

**Observed.** A tall, comparatively airy deciduous-looking tree rises from a large white rectangular planter within the desk floor. Its upper branches meet the skylight’s light opening. The raised planter has a stepped white edge. The tree, planter, skylight and surrounding chairs make one spatial arrangement; it is not a generic outdoor park tree transplanted indoors.

**Evidence.** [Tree, white planter and roof opening](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/01-6a0a9246.jpg>); [opposite view beside the bar](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/266-johnson/04-6a0a9253.jpg>).

**Reported.** Porter separately contains an indoor garden that PSF calls the Solarium, beside the kitchen and eating area. That is a different site and should not be used to name Johnson’s tree. [PSF Projects](https://www.psfprojects.com/workplace/madwell-creative-agency).

**Proposal.** Reserve a small interior clearing. Use a thin trunk and broken green clusters so staff remain readable through the canopy. Carry the skylight’s bright floor patch into the roof-off scene. This can become a shared pause point or a meeting spot without a compulsory modal. Keep Johnson’s singular tall tree distinct from Porter’s planted banquettes.

**Credit.** Johnson photographs: Matt Fry portfolio; photographer not established. PSF source supplies Porter’s Solarium description, not Johnson’s tree identification.

## 9. Coral brackets and white plywood dividers — Porter

**Observed.** A close-up shows white-faced plywood panels with exposed layered edges, a large circular hole, a coral-pink metal bracket with rounded ends and visible screws, and a slotted white metal floor support. Wide photographs show white cabinet/divider runs, circular black pulls, coloured upright posts and desk plants.

**Evidence.** [Joinery close-up](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/23-e0d74f78-3861-4549-8a54-9140639c361b.jpg>); [assembled desk floor](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/11-4-capture0005-142.jpg>).

**Reported.** PSF credits Millwright with collaborative millwork design/fabrication. Matt credits his own design and fabrication-management involvement. These broad credits do not identify the author of this one bracket.

**Proposal.** Build the desks from a white slab, a one-pixel warm ply edge, dark round pull and coloured joint/post. This is a useful assembly language for upgrades: add one desk bay or change a divider while preserving the occupied group. The bracket can also inform small functional UI connectors, but should not become a decorative border on every window.

**Credit.** Close-up photographer unspecified in Matt’s source. Wide desk-floor photograph: Zach Pontz. Architecture/millwork credits retained above.

## 10. Named rooms, saturated recesses and large paintings — Porter

**Observed.** The SUPERNOVA sign sits above a black-framed glazed meeting-room door. Amber curtains and a low broad pendant sit inside; a large painting of tightly packed, brightly coloured ghost-like figures hangs beside it and reflects in the glass. Elsewhere a MAGMA sign marks an orange-red recess; GROVE is visible on another room. The room names come from readable signage, not filenames.

**Evidence.** [SUPERNOVA, curtain and painting](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/17-10-capture0018-341.jpg>); [MAGMA recess](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/12-5-capture0001-163.jpg>); [GROVE doorway](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/19-capture0019-102.jpg>). [Coral booth sequence](</Users/juliacompton/Documents/Matt/_Mode Mode/Matt / Mode Mode/MattfryedCom/media/65porter/15-8-capture0001-251.jpg>) adds small portrait blocks and black/white globe pendants to the same room-scale vocabulary.

**Proposal.** Preserve the saturated pocket of colour within a pale timber shell. Curtains can visibly open/close when a meeting occupies the room, if the eventual mechanic supports it. Keep the painting as one concentrated block of contrasting pixel shapes; its reflection must not accidentally become a second painting. The room-name label belongs in an inspect panel at small zoom rather than illegible text across every door.

**Credit.** Photos: Zach Pontz via matched PSF gallery. Painting/portrait artists and pendant manufacturers not established; photographed artworks are references, not reusable game textures.

## Curation for the next assembled scene

- **Three major anchors:** sculpted counter at arrival, car in an open interior bay, tree beneath a skylight. They should be legible with labels hidden.
- **One changing occupation:** inflatable/shark party kit or arcade gathering, not every observed event at once.
- **Three quiet discoveries:** the tabby, stag and neon. The mint typewriter can survive as a tiny incidental object.
- **Repeatable structure:** white industrial frame, timber meeting-room volumes, saturated recesses, coral joinery, white desks. Avoid spreading the strongest object colours evenly across every building.
- **Chronology stays explicit:** Johnson’s orange exposed mezzanine stair, tree and party photos do not prove these same arrangements at Porter. Porter has its own enclosed orange stair, skylit social area and sculpted reception.
- **No trailer verified:** none of the 34 Porter and 11 Johnson gallery files inspected here establishes a trailer. A trailer would require another source or be labelled an original addition.
